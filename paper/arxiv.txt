Title:      Precision as a software choice: FP64, double-double, triple-double and beyond from the INT8 tensor cores of a laptop GPU
Authors:    Shaban Ejupi
Primary:    cs.MS (Mathematical Software)
Cross-list: cs.DC, cs.SC, math.NA
License:    CC BY 4.0
Comments:   13 pages. Code and every measurement: https://git.spacecode.tech/shaban/ozaki-blackwell

Abstract (plain text, under arXiv's 1920 characters):
Laptop GPUs run FP64 at 1/64 of their FP32 rate, while their INT8 tensor cores are hundreds of times faster: on a laptop
Blackwell GPU (RTX PRO 3000), 232 TOPS of INT8 mma.sync against 0.449 TFLOPS of FP64. Building on the Ozaki scheme II we
implement, in CUDA C and inline PTX, matrix multiplication at FP64 (5.56 TFLOPS, 12.4 times the native peak and more
accurate), double-double (1.76 TFLOPS), triple-double (1.58 TFLOPS) and up to 424-bit precision from the same INT8 units.
Ax=b with condition number 1e20 in double-double takes 0.855 s at n=8192, 499 times sooner than a __float128 LU on 24 CPU
threads, with about 270 times less energy. Exact products of integer matrices with 1,015- to 131,063-bit entries are 10 to
19 times faster than FLINT. The exact determinant of an int8 matrix comes 355 times sooner than FLINT at n=8192, with a
proved bound tight to one bit; n=32768 (425,348 bits) takes 594 s within the GPU's 12 GB. With 32- to 256-bit entries,
high-order lifting on the same GEMMs is 63 to 380 times faster than FLINT, the ratio growing with the bits. On 15 of the
most ill-conditioned real matrices of NIST Matrix Market (n up to 10,000) the certified exact solution takes 0.2-115 s,
up to ~900 times sooner than SPEX; FP64 has no correct digit on 8 of them. The same units give correctly rounded MXFP4 and BF16 products:
on real weights of Kimi K3 (2.78 trillion parameters, the largest open-weights model) every output is certified and
identical for any batch, where BF16 tensor cores with FP32 accumulation round 51-83% of them differently, and the exact
decode kernel reads 94 GB/s, 18 times the laptop's NVMe. On Nemotron 3 Ultra (560B parameters, the largest model with
published training data) the GPU's NVFP4 instruction leaves 18-49% of an expert's outputs not correctly rounded; the
correctly rounded product costs 4 times at instruction peak in prefill and nothing in decode.
