Title:      Precision as a software choice: FP64, double-double, triple-double and beyond from the INT8 tensor cores of a laptop GPU
Authors:    Shaban Ejupi
Primary:    cs.MS (Mathematical Software)
Cross-list: cs.DC, cs.SC, math.NA
License:    arXiv.org perpetual, non-exclusive license (chosen 29-10-2026)
Comments:   18 pages. Code and every measurement: https://git.spacecode.tech/shaban/ozaki-blackwell

Abstract (plain text, under arXiv's 1920 characters):
Laptop GPUs run FP64 at 1/64 of their FP32 rate, while their INT8 tensor cores are hundreds of times faster: on a laptop
Blackwell GPU (RTX PRO 3000), 232 TOPS of INT8 mma.sync against 0.449 TFLOPS of FP64. Building on the Ozaki scheme II we
implement matrix multiplication at FP64 (6.76 TFLOPS, 15.1 times the native peak and more
accurate), double-double (3.81 TFLOPS), triple-double (2.00 TFLOPS) and up to 424-bit precision from the same INT8 units.
Ax=b with condition number 1e20 in double-double takes 0.96 s at n=8192 499 times sooner than a __float128 LU on 24 CPU
threads; at n=4096 it takes about 270 times less energy. Exact products of integer matrices with 1,015- to 131,063-bit entries are 10 to
19 times faster than FLINT. The exact determinant of an int8 matrix comes 355 times sooner than FLINT at n=8192, with a
proved bound within one bit at n<=8192; n=32768 (425,348 bits) takes 594-615 s within the GPU's 12 GB. With 32- to 256-bit entries,
high-order lifting on the same GEMMs is 17 to 404 times faster than FLINT, rising with the bits. On 15 of the
most ill-conditioned real matrices of NIST Matrix Market (n to 10,000) the certified exact solution takes 0.17-115 s,
up to 7,857 times sooner than SPEX; FP64 has no correct digit on 8 of them. The same units give correctly rounded MXFP4 and BF16 products:
on real weights of Kimi K3 (2.78 trillion parameters, the largest open-weights model we found, October 2026) every output is certified,
identical for any batch, where BF16 tensor cores with FP32 accumulation round 51-83% of them differently, and the exact
decode kernel reads 94 GB/s, 18 times the laptop's NVMe. The whole of Nemotron 3 Ultra (560B) runs from
disk with every linear map correctly rounded, 19.6 s per token, as fast as FP32 accumulation, which rounds 41-47% of its
router logits differently and departs from the exact text within 29 tokens on two of three prompts.
