Title:      Precision as a software choice: FP64, double-double, triple-double and beyond from the INT8 tensor cores of a laptop GPU
Authors:    Shaban Ejupi
Primary:    cs.MS (Mathematical Software)
Cross-list: cs.DC, cs.SC, math.NA
License:    CC BY 4.0
Comments:   11 pages. Code and every measurement: https://git.spacecode.tech/shaban/ozaki-blackwell

Abstract (plain text, under arXiv's 1920 characters):
Laptop GPUs run FP64 at 1/64 of their FP32 rate, while their INT8 tensor cores are hundreds of times faster. On a laptop
Blackwell GPU (RTX PRO 3000) we measure 232 TOPS of INT8 mma.sync against 0.449 TFLOPS of FP64, a ratio of 517. Building
on the Ozaki scheme II (exact integer products of residues, reconstruction by the Chinese remainder theorem) we implement,
in CUDA C and inline PTX, matrix multiplication at FP64 (5.56 TFLOPS, 12.4 times the native peak and more accurate),
double-double (1.76 TFLOPS), triple-double (1.58 TFLOPS) and 212- to 424-bit precision from the same INT8 units. Solving
Ax=b with condition number 1e20 in double-double takes 0.855 s at n=8192, 499 times sooner than a __float128 LU on all 24
CPU threads, with about 270 times less energy; at 1e36 a triple-double LU is 32 times faster than the same arithmetic on
the CPU and 145 times faster than MPFR. With primes below 2^16 and 2^23 split into bytes, an exact product of integer
matrices with 1,015 to 131,063-bit entries is 10 to 19 times faster than FLINT on 24 threads. Turning the problem around,
int8 in and residues out, the exact determinant of an int8 matrix comes 224 times sooner than FLINT at n=4096 and 355
times at n=8192, with a proved bound on |det A| tight to one bit; n=16384 (204,480 bits) takes 66 s and n=32768
(425,348 bits) 594 s within the GPU's 12 GB. With 32- to 256-bit entries, high-order lifting on the same modular GEMMs and a proved bound for such entries are 63 to 380 times faster than FLINT (n=4096, 64-bit entries: 2.3 s against 868 s), the ratio growing with the bits. On 13 of the most ill-conditioned real matrices of NIST Matrix Market the certified exact solution takes 0.2-34 s, up to ~900 times sooner than SPEX; FP64 has no correct digit on 7 of them. Every result is bit-exact against GMP/FLINT or checked modulo independent
primes.
