\section{FP64 emulation on one laptop}\label{sec:emu}
FP64 from INT8 is not our claim, and two public implementations of it run on this laptop: cuBLAS~13's own emulation
(\texttt{CUBLAS\_COMPUTE\_64F\_EMULATED\_FIXEDPOINT}, cuBLAS 13.8.2 from NVIDIA's wheels, 8~GB workspace; its automatic dynamic
precision, ADP, or a fixed number of mantissa bits)~\cite{nvemu,cublasdoc} and GEMMul8~\cite{gemmul8} (commit 30b5b5d, INT8
backend, fast and accurate modes). We ran the three in one held window with one harness (\texttt{bench/emu\_harness.h},
\texttt{bench/fair\_jobs.sh}, 4 November 2026): the same inputs (normal values times $2^{u}$, $u$ uniform in $[-s,s]$), the error
componentwise against an exact-to-$2^{-80}$ reference at $M=N=256$, and the time as the median of five calls after a warm-up.

At FP64 accuracy cuBLAS's 55-bit mode and our 16 moduli are within a factor of two of each other's error at every spread
(0.12 and 0.24 units at $s=0$, 4.7 both at $s=16$, 31.5 both at $s=32$), and cuBLAS is $1.26\times$ faster at $n=4096$
(8.51 against 6.73~TFLOPS) and $1.91\times$ at $n=8192$, where ours falls back to 5.06~TFLOPS while cuBLAS keeps rising (we
have not profiled the fall). GEMMul8's fast mode at 16 moduli is the most accurate of the three at every spread (0.097--1.3
units) and runs at 8.29~TFLOPS at $n=4096$. Per joule, cuBLAS's 55 bits do 26\% more work than our 16 moduli and GEMMul8's
fast mode 18\% more. NVIDIA reports up to $13\times$ over native FP64 on the RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition~\cite{nvemu};
on this laptop, whose native DGEMM runs at 0.41--0.45~TFLOPS, the 55-bit emulation is $15$--$22\times$ it. For FP64 itself,
then, use theirs. What this paper adds starts past FP64: the same INT8 path at 106 to 424 bits (Section~\ref{sec:speed}) and the solves below,
where FP64 has no correct digit.
